# How to Review Automation Buying Decisions After Go-Live Without Blame Theater

Target persona: Plant leadership and buyer owner (post-project)  
Funnel stage: Adoption and nurture (learning loop)  
Core problem: postmortems become personality trials, so the organization learns nothing and repeats the same sourcing mistakes  
Main promise: a decision review method that attributes variance to system factors you can change next cycle

Go-live is not the finish line for judgment.

It is the start line for evidence about whether the buying system worked.

Blame theater feels satisfying for an afternoon.

It robs you of the next project's speed.

## Direct answer

Review automation buying decisions after go-live without blame theater by running a timeboxed session with a fixed agenda: compare pre-award predictions to observed outcomes on schedule, cost, acceptance ease, operational ownership load, and change-order volume; classify variances as brief quality, scope drift, supplier execution, plant dependency misses, or external shocks; assign system fixes (templates, gates, scorecard weights, governance cadence) instead of individual verdicts; and publish a one-page learning record with owners and due dates.

If the output names people more than it names system changes, you held a trial, not a review.

## Review agenda (sixty to ninety minutes, illustrative)

1. outcomes snapshot: what is measurably true thirty to ninety days after go-live (pick a window and keep it consistent)  
2. prediction audit: what the award record claimed about risk and schedule  
3. variance map: top five gaps between prediction and reality  
4. fix backlog: three to five changes to the buying workflow, ranked  

No open mic venting.

If someone needs venting, schedule a separate conversation.

## Variance classification (simple)

| Class | example system fix |
| --- | --- |
| brief quality | tighten challenge brief template and internal review gate |
| scope drift | stronger baseline plus change discipline after award |
| supplier execution | update evidence rules and reference depth in scorecard |
| plant dependency misses | earlier operations owner lock-in and calendar validation |
| external shock | document assumptions explicitly next cycle, do not hide in narrative |

## Checklist: signs you are in blame theater

- the meeting has no pre-read artifacts  
- scores are assigned to individuals without a process change  
- lessons are "communicate better" without a new protocol  
- procurement and engineering leave with the same tribal story as before  

## What this means for DBR77 Marketplace

DBR77 Marketplace is a workflow for automation decisions, a trust layer for integrator selection, and a system for comparing offers and reducing sourcing chaos.

A clean post-go-live review turns Marketplace-grade comparability into institutional memory.

Marketplace is not a robot catalog.

It is manufacturer-first infrastructure for repeatable improvement, not one-off heroics.

## Bottom line

The goal is not to find who was wrong.

The goal is to make the next award less lucky.
