# How to Keep Automation Supplier Governance Clean After Award

Target persona: Procurement lead with plant accountability  
Funnel stage: Adoption (post-award execution)  
Core problem: award clarity decays into informal side channels, scope drift, and weak change discipline  
Main promise: a lightweight governance rhythm that preserves comparability, approvals, and ownership without bureaucracy theater

After award, the plant is busy.

The integrator is mobilizing.

Email threads multiply.

That is when governance quietly rots.

Clean governance after award is not more meetings.

It is a small set of rules that keep decisions traceable, comparable to what you awarded, and owned by named roles.

## Direct answer

Keep supplier governance clean after award by freezing a configuration baseline, routing all scope and schedule changes through a written change path with one buyer voice, maintaining a single interface register with owners on both sides, and holding a short monthly governance review that checks only: open changes, acceptance readiness, risk owners, and escalation status.

If a decision cannot be tied to the baseline or a numbered change, treat it as informal and therefore unsafe.

## Baseline you freeze on day zero after award

Publish internally and share with the supplier:

- awarded scope statement ID and exclusions
- evaluated offer reference (what comparability was based on)
- acceptance object list version used in commercial logic
- interface register snapshot with plant and supplier owners
- communication protocol: cadence, document store, escalation ladder

This is not legal perfection.

It is operational memory.

## Change-order protocol (minimum viable)

Use a simple rule:

- any material change gets a short written record: what changed, why, cost or schedule impact, who approved on plant side, supplier acknowledgment date

Material means: touches safety, acceptance evidence, interfaces, milestones, payment triggers, or ownership.

Non-material tweaks can live in engineering notes, but they still need a single place to live so audits do not become archaeology.

## Monthly governance review (thirty to forty-five minutes)

Agenda, tight:

1. open changes: IDs, owners, target decision dates  
2. acceptance path: next object, evidence readiness, plant calendar conflicts  
3. top three risks: owner, trigger, next action  
4. escalation check: anything that should already be executive-visible  

No slide redesign.

No vendor performance storytelling unless it ties to a decision.

## What to refuse after award

Refuse:

- parallel technical decisions outside the interface register  
- procurement bypass for "speed" that skips approval mapping  
- silent scope expansion through informal chats  

Speed without records creates rework.

## What this means for DBR77 Marketplace

DBR77 Marketplace is a workflow for automation decisions, a trust layer for integrator selection, and a system for comparing offers and reducing sourcing chaos.

Post-award governance is how you keep the plant story aligned with what you compared and awarded.

Marketplace is not a robot catalog.

It is manufacturer-first infrastructure for decision hygiene from shortlist through delivery.

## Bottom line

Award is a contract event.

Governance is how you keep the contract connected to the floor.

If you cannot point to baseline plus change record for a decision, you are already paying for ambiguity.
