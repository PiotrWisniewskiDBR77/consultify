# What Buyers Should Reuse From the Last Automation Project and What Not To

Target persona: Buyer owner / program manager across multiple lines or sites  
Funnel stage: Consideration (starting the next cycle with memory)  
Core problem: teams either reinvent everything or copy the wrong artifacts and inherit hidden risk  
Main promise: a reuse map that speeds good decisions without cloning last project's mistakes

The last project left files.

Some of them are assets.

Some of them are traps dressed as templates.

Reuse should reduce sourcing chaos.

It should not smuggle outdated assumptions into a new scope and acceptance story.

## Direct answer

Reuse from the last automation project: comparability framework (scoring dimensions, weighting logic, evidence rules), interface register structure and RACI pattern, acceptance object pattern (how objects, evidence, and verifiers are written), change-order hygiene, governance cadence, and lessons-learned notes tied to decisions not emotions.

Do not reuse without refresh: site-specific mechanical assumptions, SKU mix and cycle-time baselines, safety case references tied to old layouts, commercial templates if economics or risk allocation changed, and supplier shortlists treated as automatic approvals.

## Reuse decision checklist

| Artifact | reuse | refresh first | do not reuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| weighted scorecard structure | yes | weights by project | rarely |
| actual scores from last award | no | n/a | yes |
| interface register template | yes | owners and systems | as-is snapshot |
| FAT/SAT plan skeleton | yes | objects and evidence | last project's pass/fail data |
| internal alignment one-pager format | yes | names dates thresholds | old thresholds verbatim |

If you cannot explain why an artifact still fits, it belongs in the refresh bucket.

## Step sequence: harvest memory in one working session (illustrative)

1. Pull the award baseline pack: scope ID, acceptance list version, milestone map.  
2. Pull the change log summary: top five drivers of cost or schedule movement.  
3. Interview three roles for fifteen minutes each: operations, maintenance, engineering. Ask what they would keep and what they would never repeat.  
4. Convert outputs into a "carry forward" list with owners for updates.

No workshop theater.

One tight harvest produces more signal than a retrospective slide deck.

## Comparison: asset versus liability reuse

Asset reuse speeds the next buying workflow and keeps comparability stable.

Liability reuse preserves wrong physics, wrong IT reality, or wrong commercial risk posture.

The difference is usually whether the artifact encodes a decision logic or encodes a frozen plant state.

## What this means for DBR77 Marketplace

DBR77 Marketplace is a workflow for automation decisions, a trust layer for integrator selection, and a system for comparing offers and reducing sourcing chaos.

Reusing decision structures while refreshing plant-specific facts is how manufacturers scale buying without cloning chaos.

Marketplace is not a robot catalog.

It is manufacturer-first infrastructure for repeatable comparison and award logic.

## Bottom line

Copy the discipline, not the snapshot.

If your next RFQ is mostly a find-and-replace from the last line, you are likely shipping someone else's risk forward.
