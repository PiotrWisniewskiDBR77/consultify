Score every article against these dimensions (aligned with Marketplace `01-12`):

1. Knowledge-layer fit  
   - does the article clearly serve one of the `5` product knowledge layers

2. Trust and proof  
   - practical sourcing, comparison, risk, and buying-workflow logic  
   - avoids inflated claims and vague automation rhetoric

3. Article arc  
   - real problem  
   - what actually happens  
   - common mistake or reality check  
   - what works  
   - how DBR77 approaches it (Marketplace bridge)  
   - outcome logic (bottom line)

4. Operational specificity  
   - close to plant friction, procurement mechanics, vendor comparison, and document discipline

5. Maturity and tone  
   - calm, experienced, specific, manufacturer-first, sourcing-safe

6. System fit  
   - persona, funnel stage, and LP attachment logic remain credible

## Recurring Issues In This Tranche

1. **Repeated Marketplace closing pattern**  
   Nearly every article ends with the same structural trio: `## What this means for DBR77 Marketplace`, workflow/comparability language, then `Marketplace is not a robot catalog` plus a near-identical second sentence. This matches the templating risk called out for other products and should be fixed as a **single tranche pass** (lever-keyed openers/closers per article topic), not as ten isolated edits.

2. **Structural similarity across the band**  
   Problem statement, H2 sections, tables, Marketplace bridge, bottom line: the skeleton is consistent. That is acceptable for a buying-workflow series but increases skim fatigue if bridge paragraphs stay word-identical.

3. **Conceptual adjacency (intentional overlap)**  
   `14` (brief), `15` (RFQ), and `21` (scope) sit on one clarity chain. `16` (supplier types), `17` (offer visibility), and `20` (contract) sit on another. Retrofit should add **light cross-links** where one sentence prevents duplicate reading, not merge articles.

4. **Where 13-22 already exceeds older Marketplace tone**  
   This tranche is consistently closer to the DBR77 standard than many first-wave libraries: proof-bearing checklists, explicit comparability logic, and restrained hype. The main maturity gap is **packaging repetition**, not depth.

5. **Inline production notes**  
   Repo search on English `article_EN.md` files for this band found **no** matches for the historical body-instruction compound (both casings), `[CTA`, `TODO`, `FIXME`, or `production note` patterns (same family as prior audits).
