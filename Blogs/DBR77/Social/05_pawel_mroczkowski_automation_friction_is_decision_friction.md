## Post draft

When automation projects stall, the issue is often described very generally:

"offers are hard to compare"
"the process takes too long"
"the business case is unclear"

In practice, the friction usually sits earlier:

- the challenge is weakly defined
- the choices were never tested properly
- the offers are not truly comparable
- the follow-through path is still fragmented

That is why automation friction is often a decision problem before it becomes a vendor problem.

If the setup is weak, the sourcing cycle gets expensive very quickly.

If the setup is strong, speed and comparability improve fast.
