# ROI And Budget Battlecard

Objection: We are interested, but we do not yet see a clear enough ROI or budget priority.
Typical speaker: CFO, CEO, transformation sponsor
Why it appears: the buyer sees upside but still feels weak confidence around assumptions, timing, payback logic, or internal justification.
Primary CTA: Review business case

## Bad response to avoid

Do not answer with generic promises such as:
- the ROI is always strong
- everyone is investing in AI now
- the pilot is small so there is no real risk

## Better response logic

Move the discussion from hype to decision quality.
Show that ROI confidence depends on better assumptions, stronger scenario logic, and more defensible follow-through, not only on product enthusiasm.

## Short answer

That is a fair concern.

Usually the question is not only whether the upside exists. It is whether the decision is being made on strong enough assumptions, realistic scenario logic, and a credible implementation path.

That is exactly where we try to help.

## Expanded answer

Many industrial investments underperform not because the original idea was bad, but because the decision path into approval was weaker than it looked.

That usually means:
- assumptions were fragile
- alternatives were not compared well enough
- risk was not simulated or governed clearly
- execution confidence was lower than expected

We generally suggest reviewing the business case through that lens first, because stronger decision quality tends to improve ROI confidence before anyone needs to make a larger commitment.
