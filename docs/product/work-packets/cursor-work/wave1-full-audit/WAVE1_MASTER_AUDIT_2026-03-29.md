## 3. Executive summary

Wave 1 is formally closed.

Wave 1 is not fully complete in the stronger sense of:

- full implementation completeness across every lane implied by the wider SSOT stack
- full market parity against the commercial standards named in the benchmark and readiness docs

The audit result is therefore:

- `Wave 1 closure`: `complete`
- `Wave 1 implementation completeness`: `partial and uneven`
- `Wave 1 market standard fit`: `mixed; strongest on bounded trust surfaces, weakest on full product-depth parity`

## 5. Cross-module patterns

### Pattern A: bounded closure is stronger than full product completeness

Most modules were closed on a real must-have lane:

- shell honesty
- route truth
- bounded runtime continuity
- focused regression coverage

That was enough for Wave 1 closure.

It was not enough for:

- full breadth parity
- full workflow depth
- full market-standard polish

### Pattern B: trust improved faster than depth

The strongest common improvement across the wave is honesty:

- better degraded states
- clearer recovery paths
- fewer fake empty states
- stronger boundary language

The weakest common area is depth:

- multi-step workflows
- downstream handoffs
- broader mutation parity
- full operator-control completeness

### Pattern C: business spine is credible but still split

`Inicjatywy`, `Wdrożenia`, `KPI`, and `Finanse` now form a believable bounded operating spine.

They still do not form one fully unified commercial operating system because:

- write families are uneven
- runtime truth is fragmented
- deeper workflows remain partly legacy or partly bounded

### Pattern D: visual workspace tools are real but not yet calm

`Mind map`, `Whiteboard`, `Proces flow`, and `Tabele` all have real product value.

Their shared weakness is not total absence of capability. It is:

- coherence
- interaction calmness
- one clear mental model
- parity against `Miro`, `Lucidchart`, `Airtable`, and `Coda`

## 7. Final recommendation

Formal Wave 1 closure should stand.

But product communication, planning, and future delivery should stop collapsing three different judgments into one:

- `closed`
- `implemented`
- `commercially complete`

The stronger audit verdict is:

- all 16 Wave 1 modules are `closed`
- only a small subset are close to `high implementation completeness`
- none of the weaker commercial-parity modules should be described as fully equivalent to their benchmark category leaders yet
