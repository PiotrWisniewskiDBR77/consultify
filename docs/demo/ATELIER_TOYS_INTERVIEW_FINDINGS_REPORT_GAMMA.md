## 1. Executive Takeaway

Atelier Toys is not digitally immature. The company has already built many of the ingredients expected in a serious **digital transformation**: factory telemetry, a Digital Twin program, a digital subscription product line (Atelier Digital), partner expansion through digital bundles, AI governance, OT cybersecurity investment, and a leadership group that can describe operational constraints with unusual specificity.

The issue is that these ingredients do not yet operate as one management system.

This is therefore primarily a **digital transformation diagnosis**. The findings touch the entire enterprise (operations, supply, quality, product, commercial, partners, finance, people, governance), but the connective tissue is digital: how data flows, how digital signals become decisions, how Atelier Digital monetizes, how connected products are governed, how AI and cyber controls scale, and how the management operating model becomes evidence-driven rather than slide-driven.

Interview evidence points to a company moving from entrepreneurial digitization to governed digital scaling. Teams have local tools, dashboards, pilots, and improvement programs, but the executive layer does not yet receive one integrated view of value, risk, adoption, capital allocation, and operating confidence. As a result, good digital work can still underperform because decisions arrive late, data definitions are inconsistent, and owners do not always share the same definition of progress.

The core recommendation is to move from "digital initiatives" to a managed **digital transformation operating model**. That means treating evidence, insight, initiative design, governance cadence, KPI baselines, and value tracking as one connected loop, with digital as the spine that connects every workstream.

## 3. Management Thesis

Atelier Toys is entering the **digital transformation** stage where technology value is limited less by tool availability and more by decision quality.

In the first stage of digitization, a company wins by proving that digital tools can create local performance gains. Atelier has done that. The interviews show working examples around line telemetry, quality closure, demand signals, product usage, partner activation, and cyber controls.

In the second stage, the value equation changes. The question is no longer "Can we digitize this process?" The question becomes "Can we govern many digitized processes as a digital portfolio, with reliable baselines, accountable owners, and fast escalation?" That is where Atelier is now exposed.

The value at stake is material because the gaps appear in high-leverage **digital and digitally-enabled** areas: line OEE through Digital Twin and shift-decision contracts, supplier resilience through a digital control tower, Atelier Digital subscription renewal risk through signal fusion, product roadmap allocation through evidence-based digital governance, launch readiness through cross-functional digital signals, and partner ramp quality through digital activation systems.

In other words, this is a digital transformation that happens to also be an operational, commercial, and governance program — not the other way around.

## 4. Research Design

The interview structure was designed to test both operational detail and executive readiness.

Each participant answered two series of ten questions. The first series established the baseline: where the work is today, which decisions are difficult, what data is trusted, and which risks slow execution. The second series tested maturity progression: what would improve confidence, which interventions would change behavior, where governance should tighten, and what evidence leaders would need before scaling.

This matters for a client demo because the result is not only narrative content. It is a decision artifact. The system can show how raw interviews become structured insights, how insights become initiatives, and how initiatives become a governed transformation portfolio.

## 6. The Overall Finding

Atelier Toys has a strong transformation narrative but an incomplete transformation control system.

The interviews show high ambition and credible execution energy. The company is not blocked by lack of ideas. It is blocked by the friction that appears when many good ideas compete for scarce management attention, capital, data engineering capacity, frontline adoption, and executive decision time.

The recurring pattern is visible across domains:

| Pattern | What teams experience | Management risk |
|---|---|---|
| Signals arrive late | Teams see the issue after it has already become escalation | Value leakage before intervention |
| Data definitions vary | Different teams use different success measures | Portfolio progress becomes hard to compare |
| Pilots lack scale gates | Local improvements are real but not repeatable | Pilot theater and capital dilution |
| Owners are named but dependencies are weak | Accountability exists inside functions, less across functions | Slow cross-functional execution |
| Governance is active but not yet evidence-driven | Meetings happen, but decisions do not always close the loop | Reopened decisions and delayed benefits |

## 8. Insight 1 - Factory Signals Are Not Yet Shift-Decision Signals

**Diagnosis.** Atelier has useful plant telemetry, but signal timing, event taxonomy, and supervisor action rules are not yet reliable enough for same-shift decision making. Data exists, but the operating rhythm still behaves as if many decisions are manual.

**Mechanism.** Alerts are generated, but routing, classification, and action protocols are inconsistent across shifts. The result is a 20-30 minute lag between event and corrective response, and the corrective action is not consistently captured for learning.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 0.8-1.2 M EUR annual value leakage on Line 3 from delayed same-shift response, before changeover and downtime improvements are layered in.

**Source anchor.** Marc Dubois, Plant Manager, 2026-04-19: telemetry on Line 3 is rich, but supervisors react thirty minutes after the loss has already happened.

**Decision required.** Approve a Line 3 shift-decision contract (event taxonomy + alert routing + action protocol + learning capture) before expanding sensors or software.

## 9. Insight 2 - OEE Upside Is Concentrated In Changeover And Downtime Response

**Diagnosis.** OEE loss on flagship lines is not evenly distributed. It is concentrated in changeover variation, downtime alert latency, and inconsistent response playbooks. Concentrated loss is easier to attack than diffuse inefficiency.

**Mechanism.** Three drivers compound: changeover sequence varies between shifts, alert-to-action cycle time exceeds the supervisor's decision window, and standard response playbooks are not yet consistent across teams. Each driver is digital-and-behavioral, not infrastructure-bound.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 2.4-3.6 M EUR EBITDA per line per year if Atelier moves from 62-66% OEE toward an anonymized top-quartile reference of 78-82%, plus 0.6-1.1 M EUR from changeover compression.

**Source anchor.** Operations and CTO interview cluster (Marc Dubois, Julien Moreau) confirms that OEE upside is real and concentrated.

**Decision required.** Fund the Line 3 Digital Twin as an operating-system pilot, not as a dashboard. Adoption KPIs (alert response usage, playbook adherence) must sit next to outcome KPIs.

---

## 10. Insight 3 - Quality Closure Is Not Yet A Learning Loop

**Diagnosis.** Quality teams close cases, but closure does not consistently translate into recurrence prevention, engineering feedback, or launch-readiness improvement. The gap is system design, not discipline.

**Mechanism.** Complaint, defect mode, root cause, engineering owner, corrective action, recurrence signal, and warranty exposure are not unified inside one closed-loop cockpit. The current pattern rewards case closure rather than defect elimination.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 0.9-1.6 M EUR annual cost-of-poor-quality exposure on top recurring defect families, plus warranty risk on Atelier Motion connected products as complexity scales.

**Source anchor.** Sophie Bernard, QA Director, 2026-04-21: cases close, but learning does not always follow.

**Decision required.** Approve a closed-loop quality cockpit with a recurrence KPI and a single engineering owner per defect family.

---

## 11. Insight 4 - Supplier Risk Is Visible But Not Yet Governed As Margin Exposure

**Diagnosis.** Supplier volatility is treated as a supply issue. It is in fact a margin, service, and customer-credibility issue. Signals are available, but scenario governance is still maturing.

**Mechanism.** No single weekly model connects supplier risk, buffer decisions, expected margin impact, mitigation owner, and executive escalation threshold. Trade-offs (buffer vs dual-source vs redesign vs accept-risk) happen in side conversations rather than inside a governed cockpit.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 2.0-3.5 M EUR margin and working-capital protection at stake annually, before tariff scenarios are layered in.

**Source anchor.** Isabelle Leroy, Procurement Director, 2026-04-22: supplier risk is visible, margin exposure is not.

**Decision required.** Approve a weekly supplier war room with finance escalation rights and explicit trade-off rules.

---

## 12. Insight 5 - Digital Renewal Risk Is Detected Too Late

**Diagnosis.** Atelier has enough usage data to manage Atelier Digital subscription renewal risk earlier than it does today. The constraint is signal fusion, not data availability.

**Mechanism.** Activation, feature usage, support friction, partner behavior, and account context live in separate views. Renewal risk only becomes visible after the account has already moved into escalation, when intervention cost is materially higher and recovery probability lower.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 4.0-6.0 M EUR ARR protected if Atelier moves gross renewal from 76-80% toward an anonymized B2B EdTech top-quartile reference (Peer A, Peer B, Peer C composite) of 88-92%.

**Source anchor.** Lea Martin, Customer Success Lead, 2026-04-24: a school is at renewal risk before we know it; by the time we know, we are negotiating, not curing.

**Decision required.** Launch a renewal-risk action system (prioritized intervention list with owners and playbooks), not another analytics dashboard.

## 13. Insight 6 - Product Roadmap Capital Is Not Yet Evidence-Governed

**Diagnosis.** Atelier Digital and Motion product ambition is credible, but roadmap capital is allocated through competing narratives — customer anecdotes, partner pressure, engineering capacity, executive preference, commercial urgency — rather than through repeatable evidence rules.

**Mechanism.** No standing council enforces a decision rule combining expected value, confidence level, usage evidence, implementation complexity, compliance risk, partner dependency, and renewal impact. Capital therefore flows toward the most persuasive debate rather than the strongest evidence.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 10-15% of digital product capex at risk of avoidable misallocation, equivalent to 1.5-2.5 M EUR per year of redirectable investment.

**Source anchor.** Nicolas Faure, Head of Product, 2026-04-25: roadmap allocation today is a series of persuasive debates; the work that wins is not always the work with the strongest evidence.

**Decision required.** Stand up an evidence-based roadmap council for Atelier Digital and Motion with an explicit decision rule and capital authority.

## 14. Insight 7 - Governance Exists, But Decision Memory Is Weak

**Diagnosis.** Atelier has governance forums, sponsors, and PMO capability. The weakness is not meeting frequency or attendance. It is decision memory.

**Mechanism.** Decisions are reopened because the organization does not consistently retain a crisp record of assumption, confidence level, owner, due date, evidence required, and reversal trigger. Meeting notes live in decks and inboxes; the institutional decision ledger does not yet exist.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 6-9 M EUR of unrealized portfolio value at risk over 18 months from reopened decisions, slow dependency resolution, and lost momentum.

**Source anchor.** Amelie Girard, PMO Director, 2026-05-02: governance forums are full; the weakness is decision memory.

**Decision required.** Mandate a decision ledger as a first-class transformation artifact for every Wave 1 initiative.

## 15. Insight 8 - AI Governance Is Ahead Of Many Peers, But Adoption Needs Use-Case Discipline

**Diagnosis.** Atelier has a thoughtful AI posture. The risk is not reckless AI experimentation. The risk is fragmented, low-value experimentation that consumes management attention without changing operational outcomes — and creates downside in a child-data and brand-sensitive context.

**Mechanism.** AI use cases are not yet governed through a standard economics view: decision improved, time saved, risk reduced, evidence required, human approval point, and data boundary. Without that discipline, the volume of AI work scales faster than the value or the safety posture.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** Brand, compliance, and child-data downside (COPPA, FERPA-aligned addenda, US state privacy regimes) exceeds near-term AI upside if unmanaged. Asymmetric risk profile, not a one-line EUR figure.

**Source anchor.** Julien Moreau, CTO, 2026-04-30: AI in our context is a use-case discipline question, not a volume question.

**Decision required.** Approve fewer AI use cases, but manage them with stronger evidence, explicit data boundaries, and human approval points.

## 16. Insight 9 - OT Cybersecurity Is Now A Scale Enabler

**Diagnosis.** OT cybersecurity is not only risk mitigation. For Atelier it is a prerequisite for scaling connected operations, factory telemetry, Digital Twin, and connected Atelier Motion devices. Without it, more digital connectivity becomes more operational fragility.

**Mechanism.** Asset inventory, segmentation, remote access control, monitoring, incident response rehearsal, and audit-ready evidence are not yet sequenced inside Wave 1 governance. Cyber leaders understand the risk; the executive layer has not yet connected OT hardening to transformation sequencing.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 5-10 M EUR plant disruption and customer-credibility exposure if a connected line, partner channel, or product device incident lands before controls mature.

**Source anchor.** Victor Morin, OT Security Program Lead, 2026-04-26: connecting more lines and partners before segmentation creates a faster way to be hurt.

**Decision required.** Treat OT cyber hardening as a Wave 1 dependency, not a later compliance topic. Milestones must include asset inventory, segmentation, remote access controls, monitoring, and incident response rehearsal.

## 17. Insight 10 - Partner Expansion Needs A First-90-Day Activation System

**Diagnosis.** Partner growth can accelerate US revenue, but it also creates execution variability. Partner activation must be governed with the same rigor as internal transformation work, especially for US districts and GPO-led procurement.

**Mechanism.** The first 90 days are decisive. Today, partners are signed faster than they are onboarded; proof assets, usage signals, training completion, pipeline contribution, support routes, and escalation rules are not yet packaged into a repeatable activation system.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 2.0-3.0 M EUR pipeline contribution at stake if Atelier moves first-90-day partner productivity from ~50% toward an anonymized top channel-program reference of 75%.

**Source anchor.** Zoe Perrin, Partner Program Manager, 2026-04-29: we can sign US distributors faster than we can make them productive.

**Decision required.** Approve a first-90-day partner activation scorecard with shared milestones, proof assets, and productivity cohorts.

## 18. Insight 11 - Finance Is Ready To Move From Budget Control To Value Control

**Diagnosis.** Finance is positioned to be a transformation accelerator, not only a budget gatekeeper. The organization is ready for confidence bands, initiative-level ROI assumptions, scenario tracking, and benefits realization discipline, but the operating standard is not yet in place.

**Mechanism.** The current financial lens answers "Did we spend according to plan?" instead of "Are we learning whether this value pool is real?" Without baselines, targets, owner accountability, and periodic value re-forecasting, capex, opex, adoption cost, and delayed benefits cannot be reconciled at executive level.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** Up to 30% of value claims could prove uncollectible at audit if baselines and confidence bands are not set before Gate 2 funding.

**Source anchor.** Claire Laurent, CFO and Head of People, 2026-04-28: finance is ready to move from spend control to value control; we need baselines, confidence levels, and the right to stop.

**Decision required.** Require confidence-banded value cases, baseline owners, and stop rights for every Wave 1 initiative before Gate 2 funding.

## 19. Insight 12 - People Adoption Is The Hidden Constraint

**Diagnosis.** Most Wave 1 value depends on frontline behavior: supervisors acting on alerts, quality teams using recurrence data, customer success following renewal playbooks, partners adopting proof assets, managers trusting new dashboards. Adoption is a transformation risk, not a communications task.

**Mechanism.** Initiatives currently treat adoption as training and rollout. Roles are not consistently redesigned, adoption KPIs are not embedded in initiative scorecards, and management action when adoption stalls is not predefined. Technology changes routines before routines are managed.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 1.5-2.5 M EUR Wave 1 KPI slippage if adoption is not measured at the role level (alert response usage, playbook adherence, partner proof-asset usage).

**Source anchor.** Learning experience and PMO interview cluster (Lea Rousseau, Amelie Girard).

**Decision required.** Embed adoption KPIs (role-specific) into every Wave 1 initiative scorecard, with management action triggers when adoption stalls.

## 20. Insight 13 - Data Governance Is The Portfolio's Common Dependency

**Diagnosis.** Almost every Wave 1 opportunity depends on reliable, shared data definitions. OEE, defect recurrence, supplier risk, renewal risk, partner activation, roadmap value, and AI use-case performance all require a single source of truth.

**Mechanism.** Definitions, ownership, lineage, and data-quality rules are inconsistent across functions. The boring work (taxonomy, owner assignment, quality SLAs) is underfunded relative to digital pilots, which means executive governance risks becoming opinion-based rather than fact-based.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 8-12% portfolio productivity drag from inconsistent definitions and reconciliation rework, plus rising COPPA / FERPA-aligned audit cost as US scale grows.

**Source anchor.** Industrial Data Lead evidence cluster across operations, customer success, and product interviews.

**Decision required.** Fund top-25 metric ownership and lineage in Wave 1; treat data governance as enabling infrastructure, not a back-office project.

## 21. Insight 14 - The PMO Must Become A Transformation Control Tower

**Diagnosis.** The PMO cannot remain a reporting office if Atelier wants to scale transformation value. It must become the operating layer that connects insights, initiatives, owners, dependencies, decisions, KPIs, risks, and evidence — and acts on them.

**Mechanism.** Today's cadence consolidates slides; it does not consistently resolve dependencies, force scale/stop decisions, or compress executive decision time. As complexity rises, an under-mandated PMO becomes a portfolio bottleneck.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 6-9 M EUR of unrealized value risk over 18 months from overloaded portfolio and slow decision resolution; same root cause as Insight 7 but a different management lever.

**Source anchor.** Amelie Girard, PMO Director, 2026-05-02; reinforced across CEO, CFO, and Plant Manager testimony.

**Decision required.** Reposition the PMO as a Transformation Control Tower with weekly execution control, monthly executive value review, and explicit decision rights.

## 22. Insight 15 - Launch Readiness Needs A Cross-Functional Signal

**Diagnosis.** New product and digital launch readiness depends on product, engineering, quality, supply, customer success, partner enablement, and cyber readiness. Each function has a checklist; the executive layer does not have one cross-functional signal answering the only question that matters: can we launch without creating preventable downstream issues?

**Mechanism.** Readiness checks are local. Defect risk, supply confidence, support readiness, partner enablement, cybersecurity review, and customer-facing evidence are not yet integrated into a single launch cockpit. Launch debt accumulates silently.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** Avoidable launch debt and customer friction risk in US expansion (Atelier Motion connectivity, Atelier Digital district contracts), with downstream warranty and renewal exposure.

**Source anchor.** Cross-cluster synthesis: Product, QA, Procurement, Customer Success, OT Security interviews.

**Decision required.** Build a launch readiness cockpit after Wave 1 signal quality matures (data governance + closed-loop quality + OT cyber), so the cockpit is fed by clean signals from day one.

## 23. Insight 16 - Customer Success Is Becoming A Strategic Data Function

**Diagnosis.** Customer Success is no longer only a service function. In Atelier's digital future it is the function closest to usage, adoption friction, renewal risk, partner enablement, and product feedback — a strategic data function in disguise.

**Mechanism.** Usage patterns, feature drop-off, ticket themes, training completion, and expansion signals are not yet integrated into the executive evidence base. The result is that digital growth is managed using lagging commercial indicators (renewal lost / won) rather than leading adoption indicators.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 4.0-6.0 M EUR ARR protection (same value pool as Insight 5) plus 1.0-1.5 M EUR avoided support cost is contingent on Customer Success data quality and integration.

**Source anchor.** Lea Martin, Customer Success Lead, 2026-04-24; cross-referenced with Product and Partner Program interviews.

**Decision required.** Integrate Customer Success signals into roadmap governance and renewal-risk action system as a first-class data source, not a downstream feedback loop.

## 24. Insight 17 - Commercial Funnel Signals Are Fragmented

**Diagnosis.** Pipeline, partner, campaign, subscription, and customer success signals are not yet fused into one commercial signal layer. Management cannot reliably distinguish true growth momentum from noisy activity.

**Mechanism.** Each commercial system reports its own success metric. Volume, quality, conversion, retention, and margin contribution are not separated. The result is that not all revenue growth is governed equally: a low-retention partner deal can look identical to a high-retention digital subscription cohort.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** 2-3 M EUR partner and digital pipeline quality at stake annually; growth margin diluted by deals with weak onboarding, high support cost, and low renewal probability.

**Source anchor.** Revenue operations interview cluster, cross-validated with Partner Program and Customer Success.

**Decision required.** Build a commercial signal layer separating volume / quality / conversion / retention / margin, before scaling US partner growth.

## 25. Insight 18 - Strategic Narrative Debt Is Diluting Capital And Decision Speed

**Diagnosis.** Atelier Toys is carrying strategic narrative debt. The company runs multiple valid but competing stories — better factories, stronger quality, more resilient supply, digital subscription growth, safer AI, better partner activation, stronger governance — without one explicit, board-endorsed digital transformation thesis. This is not a communications gap. It is a capital allocation and decision-speed gap.

**Mechanism.** When the executive thesis is plural, three structural distortions emerge: (1) capital is allocated through narrative competition rather than evidence rules, reinforcing Insight 6; (2) governance forums spend time re-debating priorities rather than resolving dependencies, reinforcing Insights 7 and 14; (3) external stakeholders (US distributors, US districts, partners, talent market) cannot rehearse a single message back to the company, which dilutes both demand-side trust and internal cohesion.

**Quantified consequence (demo basis).** Compounding multiplier on the other 17 insights. Conservatively, 10-15% of total Wave 1 value pool is at risk of leakage if the program is described in three or four different ways across the executive committee, board, plant, partners, and US buyers. On a Wave 1 envelope of approximately 14-22 M EUR run-rate value, that is 1.4-3.3 M EUR of avoidable dilution per year.

**Source anchor.** CEO, CFO, PMO interview synthesis (Antoine Laurent 2026-04-18, Claire Laurent 2026-04-28, Amelie Girard 2026-05-02), reinforced by external readiness factors in Section 5A.

**Decision required.** Adopt one explicit board narrative: Atelier Toys is running a governed digital transformation that converts distributed digital signals into reliable enterprise decisions, with measurable value in operations, digital revenue quality, and enterprise risk. Every Wave 1 initiative must be expressible as a sub-thesis of that one story.
