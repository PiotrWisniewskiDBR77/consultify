## 1. Executive Summary

Atelier Toys has moved past the stage where the main question is whether **digital transformation** is needed. The interview and portfolio evidence show that the company already has meaningful digital assets: plant telemetry, Digital Twin ambition, supplier risk signals, quality improvement programs, the Atelier Digital subscription platform, connected Motion devices, partner expansion through digital bundles, AI governance, OT cyber investment, and a leadership team that understands the operational stakes.

This program is therefore framed explicitly as a **digital transformation** of Atelier Toys. The work spans the whole organization (operations, supply, quality, product, commercial, partners, finance, people, governance), but the connective tissue is digital: digital signals, digital decisions, digital products, digital governance, and digitally-enabled adoption.

The management challenge is orchestration. Too much value is still dependent on local judgment, manual reconciliation, delayed escalation, and inconsistent digital evidence standards. This creates a familiar digital transformation risk: many digital initiatives look sensible on their own, but the enterprise does not fully convert them into measurable value.

Recommendation: launch a 90-day Digital Transformation Control Tower with 5-7 Wave 1 initiatives, while using DRD to mature the full portfolio. The goal is not another planning cycle. The goal is to make the organization measurably better at turning digital evidence into decisions, decisions into initiatives, and initiatives into value.

## 3. Strategic Diagnosis

Atelier's transformation is constrained by four management gaps:

| Gap | What it means | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence fragmentation | Signals exist, but they sit across systems, functions, and routines | Executives cannot compare value and risk consistently |
| Decision latency | Teams know issues before governance converts them into decisions | Benefits are delayed and risks become escalations |
| Adoption variability | Tools are live before role routines are fully redesigned | Technology deployment does not equal business impact |
| Value ambiguity | Budgets are clearer than realized benefits | Capital allocation can reward activity instead of value |

The program should therefore be built around management behavior, not only technology delivery. Technology creates the signal. Governance converts the signal into action. Value tracking proves whether the action worked.

## 4. North Star

Atelier Toys should define the **digital transformation** North Star as:

**A connected, digitally-governed operating model that improves factory reliability, accelerates profitable digital growth (Atelier Digital subscriptions, Motion connectivity, partner bundles), and reduces enterprise risk by converting distributed digital signals into governed management decisions.**

This North Star is deliberately cross-functional and explicitly digital. It avoids four traps:

- It is broader than factory modernization alone.
- It is more disciplined than a generic digital-growth ambition.
- It treats cybersecurity, data governance, and adoption as value enablers rather than compliance side topics.
- It frames the program as a digital transformation rather than a vague "operational program," which is the framing that lands credibly with US executive audiences and US distributor governance committees.

The North Star also gives the executive team a consistent test for initiative approval: does this work improve reliability, profitable digital growth, or digital risk control through better evidence and faster decisions?

## 7. Stage 2 Design Principle

Stage 2 should be managed as a 90-day value-validation sprint, not a transformation theater exercise.

The key design principle is simple: every Wave 1 initiative must have a baseline, target, accountable business owner, execution owner, decision cadence, risk register, and evidence standard before it receives executive attention.

This prevents three predictable problems:

| Failure mode | How Stage 2 prevents it |
|---|---|
| Too many initiatives compete for attention | Wave 1 limited to 5-7 priorities |
| Benefits are claimed without proof | Baseline and target required before scale |
| Risks are escalated late | Weekly control tower with decision ledger |

## 13. KPI Architecture

Atelier should use a three-level KPI architecture.

| Level | Purpose | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome KPIs | Measure business value | OEE, churn risk, defect recurrence, margin exposure |
| Adoption KPIs | Measure behavior change | Alert response usage, playbook adherence, dashboard adoption |

This structure prevents a common error: treating project completion as value realization. A system can go live without changing outcomes. A dashboard can be adopted without improving decisions. Governance must measure all three levels.

## 20A. Org Design And Decision Rights

A transformation operating model must declare who decides what. The recommended design adds the minimum number of new roles and explicit authorities.

| Role | Mandate | Reports to | Decision authority | Headcount delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chief Transformation Officer (Antoine Laurent's delegate; Amelie Girard interim) | Owns the Transformation Control Tower; chairs weekly governance | CEO | Resolves dependency conflicts up to 0.5 M EUR; escalates above | 0 (existing PMO Director repositioned) |
| Wave 1 Initiative Leads | One per Wave 1 initiative; combine business and execution ownership | CTO + sponsoring exec | Approve task scope and adoption plan; escalate KPI variance above defined thresholds | 0 (existing leaders) |
| Evidence Owners | Maintain KPI definition, source, lineage, confidence | Industrial Data Lead | Block initiative funding gates if baseline is missing | +1 FTE (Industrial Data Lead team) |

| US Market Liaison | Connects partner activation, COPPA posture, and US procurement readiness to the program | Chief Transformation Officer | Approves US-specific evidence packages and partner readiness gates | +1 FTE (or contract) once US Wave begins |

Total proposed headcount delta is 2-3 FTE, plus mandate refresh on existing roles. This is intentionally light. The program is designed to be governance-led, not headcount-led.

| Decision class | Decision rights |
|---|---|
| Approve Wave 1 scope and funding | Executive Committee (CEO, CFO, CTO, sponsoring leads) |

Putting decision rights in writing is the single most underrated step. It removes "we will discuss" from governance vocabulary.

## 21. Initiative Portfolio Logic

The portfolio should be managed through four categories.

| Category | Definition | Management action |
|---|---|---|
| Execute now | Strong evidence, clear value, accountable owner | Fund and govern weekly |
| Prepare next | Good idea, dependencies not ready | Build prerequisites |
| Discover | Promising but baseline weak | Keep in draft with specific evidence tasks |
| Stop / close | Low value, duplicate, or completed | Remove from active executive agenda |

This classification helps the executive team avoid treating all initiatives equally. Equal attention is not fairness; it is poor capital allocation.

## 22. Funding Logic

The first 90 days should use staged funding.

| Funding gate | What must be true |
|---|---|
| Gate 1 - Discovery | Problem statement, owner, evidence gap, next proof task |
| Gate 2 - Pilot | Baseline, target, decision owner, adoption plan |
| Gate 3 - Scale | KPI movement, adoption evidence, risk controls, ROI confidence |
| Gate 4 - Institutionalize | Process owner, control metrics, BAU handoff |

This keeps the company from overfunding attractive narratives before evidence exists, while still allowing speed where evidence is already strong.

## 23A. Contrarian View

Most external advisors will recommend that Atelier accelerate AI adoption, expand the Wave 1 portfolio, and pursue a broader connected-product strategy in the US.

This program disagrees on three points and states the disagreement explicitly so that the executive committee can debate it consciously.

| Position | What most advisors will say | What this program recommends | Why we disagree |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI scale | Run more AI pilots quickly to capture an AI-native narrative | Approve fewer AI use cases, governed harder; defer broad AI rollout to Wave 2 | In a children's STEM context, COPPA and brand sensitivity make a single bad AI use case more expensive than the upside of three good ones. Use-case discipline is the moat. |
| Portfolio breadth | Launch 10-12 initiatives to demonstrate transformation ambition | Hold Wave 1 to 5-7 priorities, with the rest in disciplined Wave 2 candidacy | Atelier's bottleneck is not idea generation. It is decision throughput. Adding initiatives drops average decision quality. |
| Connectivity expansion | Connect more lines and Motion devices fast to fuel the data flywheel | Connect only after OT cyber and data governance reach a defined floor | Connecting before controls are ready creates a faster way to be hurt without creating a faster way to learn. |

Naming the contrarian wedge is itself a governance act. It tells the board that the program has a point of view, not only a portfolio.
