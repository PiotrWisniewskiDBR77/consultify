## Slide 7 - Insight 1: Charlotte has the data, but not in real time

Headline: Plant performance signals exist in machines and a SQL database, but reach decision-makers only after the fact, locking the site into reactive management.

Confidence: HIGH (4 of 5 respondents: Finance, Eng/Maint, Plant/Ops, Production).

Direct evidence (verbatim from APLIX answers):

- Eng/Maint lead: "We rely heavily on manual reporting after the fact. We do collect quite a bit of data, but are not currently using it for real-time dashboards. This is a current project we would like to get moving."
- Eng/Maint lead: "Real time production status is a problem today... we have the data. We are just not visually showing it. Most production decisions are made after the fact."
- Eng/Maint lead: "I would really like to see OEE and scrap reported real-time. We currently do not calculate an OEE. We track scrap, but it is after the fact."
- Plant/Ops lead: "Manually-generated KPIs are used to track plant performance. We have set the stage for Dashboards using line data... This is in the initial stages."
- Plant/Ops lead: "Pretty much all KPIs in manufacturing are manually driven."
- Production lead: "Performance gets signals from machines that are then manually reported."
- Production lead: "Visually it is not good other than seeing line running and handwritten information."
- Production lead: "Daily reports that are hard to find."
- Finance lead: "The financials require manual reconciliation in all segments of the monthly financial activity to ensure completeness and accuracy."
- Finance lead (near-perfect day): "...start the day with accurate, up-to-date financial and operational data we can trust."

Mechanism: data is captured in machines and historians, but the visualization / dashboarding layer is largely absent. Decisions wait for end-of-shift, daily, or month-end batch reports.

Business consequence: management runs in firefighting mode; corrective actions arrive after the loss is already booked; scrap stays flat even when changes are made (explicitly observed by Eng/Maint lead).

## Slide 8 - Insight 2: The 980 (and HOS) extrusion lines concentrate the risk

Headline: A small set of named extrusion assets - the 980 lines, HOS, and adjacent extrusion areas - drives a disproportionate share of scrap, downtime, and quality risk, and is already known to leadership.

Confidence: HIGH (3 of 5 respondents independently named 980 as the top concern; capital investment in dies is already in flight).

Direct evidence (verbatim):

- Plant/Ops lead: "The biggest impact on the bottom line are the Extrusion KPIs for scrap and efficiency (980, HOS and Packaging). 980 and HOS are regularly off target."
- Plant/Ops lead (biggest 6-12 month risk): "980 and HOS scrap and quality issues."
- Plant/Ops lead: "Currently the 980 process is not meeting capacity due to die issues that are being addressed through capital investments."
- Eng/Maint lead: "Our 980 lines are probably the most concerning because of age and production demand."
- Eng/Maint lead: "We have recently started to collect process data [for 980 lines], but it is not readily available... If we could understand this data, it may help us in reducing scrap and improving our efficiency on those lines."
- Eng/Maint lead (frustration): "We have made several changes over the last couple of years that should have had a direct positive effect on scrap. The results do not show that. They really just show the scrap levels to be flat."
- Eng/Maint lead (die changeover bottleneck): "Our die assembly and pre-heat oven is a bottleneck for changing dies. Die preparation is a time consuming process... We can also only handle one production line at a time. If we need to start-up 2 lines, there is a time delay."
- Production lead (biggest plant bottleneck): "980 runtime where bad productivity leads to more scrap and less shipments to customer."
- Production lead: "Scrap in all areas of extrusion including stamp, 980, IMH and packaging."

Mechanism: aging extrusion asset base + die-preparation throughput limit + process data captured but unanalysed + capital investment underway but not yet paying back.

Business consequence: capacity loss, scrap that does not respond to interventions, shipment risk.

## Slide 9 - Insight 3: Work-order accuracy and cycle counts drain leadership time

Headline: Transaction integrity gaps in work orders and cycle counts force a permanent manual Finance - Operations reconciliation loop, named by three leaders as the single best one-quarter improvement target.

Confidence: HIGH (3 of 5 respondents independently named work-order / cycle-count as #1; one explicitly chose it as the single-process priority for the next quarter).

Direct evidence (verbatim):

- Finance lead (single-process priority next quarter): "I would focus on work order transaction accuracy and cycle count discipline. These two processes directly affect inventory valuation, cost of goods sold, and the reliability of financial reporting."
- Finance lead: "Misstated inventory and production costs, requiring manual adjustments and write-offs."
- Finance lead: "Reconciliations between Finance and Operations related to correctness of daily transactions."
- Finance lead: "Finance spends too much time validating inputs. The team operates reactively instead of proactively."
- Plant/Ops lead (single-process priority next quarter): "Work Order Accuracy and Monthly Cycle Counting resulting in more accurate inventory counts."
- Plant/Ops lead: "Work order and cycle counting issues that require much time and effort to investigate and reconcile."
- Plant/Ops lead: "Work orders and inventory issues because it takes a lot of managers' time away from other issues."
- Production lead: "Counting product and reconciling discrepancies."

Mechanism: transaction inaccuracy at the shop floor + monthly cycle-count cadence + Finance-Operations boundary => sustained manual reconciliation burden.

Business consequence: inventory misstatement, COGS volatility, financial reporting friction, and a recurring loss of leadership capacity.

## Slide 11 - Initiative cards (Wave-1)

### Initiative 1 - Real-Time Plant KPI Dashboards

- Scope: live dashboards for OEE per extrusion line, scrap by line (980, HOS, IMH, Packaging), unplanned downtime, throughput.
- Foundation in place: process data already captured in a SQL database (Eng/Maint lead); Historian tags on newer machines (Plant/Ops lead); dashboards described as "in the initial stages" (Plant/Ops lead).
- 90-day milestone: a live OEE + scrap dashboard for the 980 line, refreshing intra-shift.
- KPIs: % decisions backed by real-time data, dashboard refresh latency, OEE methodology adopted (today: OEE not calculated).

### Initiative 2 - 980 Line Performance Recovery

- Scope: analyse the existing SQL process data for scrap drivers; coordinate with the in-flight capital die investment; reduce die changeover time; protect against power blink impact (Plant/Ops lead: ~12-15/year).
- Foundation in place: capital investment underway; process data already collected; specific die / pre-heat oven bottleneck named.
- 90-day milestone: scrap-driver analysis complete; baseline OEE calculated; die changeover time baselined; one measurable improvement deployed.
- KPIs: 980 scrap rate, 980 OEE, die changeover hours, unplanned downtime hours.

### Initiative 3 - Work Order & Cycle Count Integrity Program

- Scope: daily / weekly cycle count cadence; work-order transaction accuracy controls; Finance - Operations daily reconciliation review.
- Foundation in place: monthly cycle count process already in operation; Finance team already reconciling manually.
- 90-day milestone: daily cycle count cadence stood up; transaction accuracy baseline measured; monthly reconciliation hours baselined.
- KPIs: cycle count accuracy %, inventory variance value/month, reconciliation hours/month, manual adjustment count/month.

## Slide 12 - 90-day execution plan and risks

### Sequence

- Weeks 1-2: lock baselines (OEE methodology, scrap definitions, cycle-count cadence); name owners; agree IT support model.
- Weeks 3-6: pilot 980 dashboard; launch 980 scrap-driver analysis; launch daily cycle count cadence.
- Weeks 7-12: extend dashboards to HOS, IMH, and Packaging; lock 980 driver hypotheses; lock reconciliation reduction targets.

### Risks called out by respondents (not invented)

- Power blink frequency (Plant/Ops lead: ~12-15/yr; IT lead: 6-10/yr) - impacts Hygiene extrusion. Mitigation: include power blink count in dashboard, escalate to facilities.
- Raw material change protocol bypass (Plant/Ops lead) - quality risk. Mitigation: protect change-management protocol in Wave-1 governance.
- Spare parts and resin compatibility (Eng/Maint lead; Production lead "Not finding compatible resins for the packaging machines") - supply fragility. Mitigation: parts and resin matrix on dashboard.
- IT 3rd-party support latency and timezone (IT lead) - escalation slowness. Mitigation: include IT escalation owner in governance cadence.
- Coverage gap: no Quality, Supply Chain, Sales / CS, HR voice in current dataset. Mitigation: extend pack to those roles in Wave-2 design phase.
