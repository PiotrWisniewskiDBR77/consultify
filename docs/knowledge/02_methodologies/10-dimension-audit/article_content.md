# The 10-Dimension Audit: A Complete Guide to Organizational Maturity Assessment

*Reading time: 6 minutes*

---

## Beyond Subjective Assessments

Traditional maturity assessments suffer from a fundamental problem: they're subjective.

Different assessors give different scores. Different companies interpret criteria differently. The result is a number that looks precise but means very little.

The 10-Dimension Audit changes this. It provides objective, evidence-based scoring across every operational area — with clear criteria that anyone can apply consistently.

## The 10 Dimensions

Every manufacturing organization — regardless of industry — operates across the same fundamental dimensions:

| # | Dimension | What It Covers |
|---|-----------|----------------|
| 1 | **Quality Management** | QMS maturity, defect rates, inspection processes, customer complaints |
| 2 | **Production Planning** | Scheduling accuracy, capacity utilization, changeover efficiency |
| 3 | **Supply Chain** | Supplier reliability, inventory optimization, procurement processes |
| 4 | **Maintenance** | CMMS usage, preventive/predictive maintenance, MTBF/MTTR |
| 5 | **Workforce & Skills** | Skills matrix, training programs, certification levels |
| 6 | **Safety & Environment** | HSE compliance, incident rates, environmental management |
| 7 | **IT Infrastructure** | System integration, data availability, cybersecurity |
| 8 | **Continuous Improvement** | Lean maturity, suggestion systems, Kaizen implementation |
| 9 | **Leadership & Change** | Change management capability, transformation experience |
| 10 | **Performance Management** | KPI systems, dashboard usage, goal alignment |

These dimensions are interconnected. Excellence in one often depends on capability in others. That's why we assess all ten.

## The Scoring System

Each dimension is scored on a 1-10 scale with specific, observable criteria:

### Level 1-3: Reactive

- No standardized processes
- Problems addressed as they occur
- Limited documentation
- Tribal knowledge dominates

### Level 4-6: Defined

- Processes documented but inconsistently followed
- Some data collection, limited analysis
- Improvement efforts are ad-hoc
- Moderate management attention

### Level 7-8: Managed

- Standardized processes consistently applied
- Data-driven decision making
- Systematic continuous improvement
- Strong management commitment

### Level 9-10: World-Class

- Predictive and preventive capabilities
- Benchmark performance in industry
- Innovation leadership
- Best practice sharing externally

The scoring isn't comparative — it's absolute. A score of 6 means the same thing whether you're a 50-person shop or a Fortune 500 plant.
