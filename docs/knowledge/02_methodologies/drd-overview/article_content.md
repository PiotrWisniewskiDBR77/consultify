## The Problem with Traditional Transformation

Every year, organizations invest billions in digital transformation. Yet studies consistently show that 70% of these initiatives fail to deliver their promised value. 

Why? Because most approaches focus on **technology** rather than **constraints**.

Teams implement new systems. Consultants deliver PowerPoint decks. Vendors sell licenses. But the underlying problems — the real barriers to improvement — remain untouched.

## Introducing DRD: Demand, Resources, Dependencies

The DRD methodology takes a different approach. Instead of starting with solutions, it starts with a clear understanding of three fundamental dimensions:

### 1. Demand

**What does your market, strategy, and customers actually require?**

Demand includes:
- Customer expectations for quality, speed, and cost
- Strategic priorities set by leadership
- Regulatory and compliance requirements
- Competitive pressures

Most organizations have a reasonable understanding of Demand. It's the "what we want to achieve" side of the equation.

### 2. Resources

**What capacity do you actually have to meet that demand?**

Resources include:
- Equipment and technology
- Workforce skills and availability
- Financial capital
- Time and management attention

Here's where problems begin. Organizations often overestimate their Resources or assume capacity constraints will magically resolve themselves during implementation.

### 3. Dependencies

**What hidden connections create friction between Resources and Demand?**

Dependencies are the most overlooked dimension:
- Scheduling dependencies between departments
- Data flows between systems
- Approval chains and decision rights
- Cultural norms and unwritten rules

Dependencies explain why a "simple" change takes months instead of days. They're the invisible friction in your organization.

## The 10-Dimension Assessment

DRD becomes actionable through a structured assessment across 10 operational dimensions:

| Dimension | Focus Area |
|-----------|------------|
| Quality Management | QMS maturity, defect rates, inspection processes |
| Production Planning | Scheduling accuracy, capacity utilization |
| Supply Chain | Supplier reliability, inventory optimization |
| Maintenance | CMMS usage, preventive vs. reactive maintenance |
| Workforce | Skills matrix, training programs |
| Safety & Environment | HSE compliance, incident rates |
| IT Infrastructure | System integration, data availability |
| Continuous Improvement | Lean maturity, suggestion systems |
| Leadership | Change management capability |
| Performance Management | KPI systems, dashboard usage |

Each dimension is scored 1-10 based on evidence, not opinion. The result is a clear maturity map that shows exactly where you stand.

## From Assessment to Action

Once the assessment is complete, Consultinity's AI analyzes the gaps and generates prioritized initiatives. Each initiative is ranked by:

- **Impact**: How much will this improve your operation?
- **Feasibility**: Given your current Resources, can you actually do this?
- **Dependencies**: What other changes need to happen first?

This prevents the common trap of pursuing high-impact initiatives that have impossible dependencies.

## Real-World Example

An automotive supplier approached us with a familiar challenge: OEE was stuck at 68% despite years of "optimization" efforts.

Traditional analysis would have focused on equipment reliability or operator training. Instead, the DRD assessment revealed something different.

The constraint wasn't equipment. It was a **scheduling dependency** between three departments. Each department optimized locally, but their schedules conflicted — creating daily firefighting that consumed management attention and caused changeover delays.

Addressing this single dependency — through a simple shared scheduling protocol — improved OEE to 80% in 8 weeks. No new equipment. No expensive software. Just clarity about where the real constraint was.
