# Automotive Plant Transformation: 68% to 80% OEE in 8 Weeks

*Reading time: 5 minutes*

---

## The Challenge

A European Tier 1 automotive supplier producing brake components faced a persistent problem: **OEE stuck at 68%**.

The quality was excellent — customer reject rates below 10 ppm. The equipment was modern. The workforce was skilled and engaged.

Yet efficiency remained stubbornly below target. Over three years, the plant had invested in:

- New CNC machines
- Operator training programs
- Maintenance improvement initiatives
- Scheduling software
- Lean consulting

Each initiative showed short-term gains. Each faded within months. The plant manager was skeptical when approached about yet another improvement methodology.

At first glance, nothing jumped out. The plant was reasonably mature across all dimensions.

## The Hidden Dependency

The DRD analysis went deeper. We mapped not just capabilities, but **dependencies** between departments.

What we found:

The plant had three main production areas: **Stamping**, **Assembly**, and **Finishing**. Each department had its own scheduling logic:

- Stamping optimized for long runs to minimize changeovers
- Assembly scheduled to customer ship dates
- Finishing prioritized urgent orders first

**The result?** Constant conflict.

Every morning, the floor supervisor spent 2-3 hours playing "traffic cop" — manually adjusting what ran when, negotiating with department heads, expediting critical orders, and managing WIP that accumulated at handoff points.

This wasn't visible in the OEE data. Each department's availability and performance looked reasonable. But the **system** was losing 10-15% of capacity to coordination overhead.

## The Solution

The intervention was surprisingly simple.

### Implemented Changes:

1. **Weekly Master Scheduling Meeting** (30 min)
   - All three department heads + planning
   - One shared schedule for the week
   - Clear priority rules for conflicts

2. **Pull-Based Handoff Protocol**
   - Assembly pulls from stamping (no WIP push)
   - Visual signals for replenishment
   - Maximum WIP limits at each buffer

3. **Shared KPIs**
   - Replaced departmental targets with flow metrics
   - Measure throughput at final step, not intermediate stages

### What We Didn't Do:

- No new scheduling software
- No equipment changes
- No headcount additions
- No major capital expenditure

## The Results

Within **8 weeks**, OEE reached **80%**.

| Metric | Before | After | Change |
|--------|--------|-------|--------|
| OEE | 68% | 80% | +12 points |
| Changeover delays | 45 min avg | 18 min avg | -60% |
| WIP levels | 2.3 days | 0.8 days | -65% |
| Supervisor firefighting | 2.5 hr/day | 0.5 hr/day | -80% |
| On-time delivery | 87% | 96% | +9 points |

The 12-point OEE improvement came almost entirely from:
- **Availability gains**: Fewer unplanned changeovers from schedule conflicts
- **Performance gains**: Reduced micro-stops waiting for upstream material

Quality remained unchanged — it was already excellent.

## Key Learnings

### 1. Dependencies Are Invisible to Traditional Analysis

Standard OEE tracking looks at each machine or line independently. It doesn't capture the friction between departments that accumulates across the value stream.

### 2. Simple Fixes Often Outperform Technology

The plant's initial instinct was to buy better scheduling software. But the problem wasn't algorithmic — it was organizational. A 30-minute weekly meeting solved what a six-figure software investment could not.

### 3. DRD Methodology Exposes What Data Misses

By mapping Resources and Dependencies alongside Demand, we identified the constraint that years of OEE data had hidden. The issue wasn't the equipment or the people — it was the coordination between them.

## Conclusion

This case demonstrates a pattern we see frequently: **mature operations blocked by hidden dependencies**.

The plant had optimized within silos. Each department was performing well by its own metrics. But the system was losing significant capacity to coordination friction.

The DRD assessment identified this dependency. The solution required no technology investment — just alignment.
