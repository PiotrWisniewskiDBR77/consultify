## Core Content (120 sec)

### The Situation (30 sec)

"This company makes brake components for major OEMs. Quality is perfect — they have to be. But efficiency was killing margins.

Management had tried everything: new equipment, operator training, maintenance improvement, scheduling software. Each initiative showed short-term gains that faded within months.

When they came to us, they were skeptical. Another consulting engagement with another set of recommendations."

### The Discovery (40 sec)

"We ran the 10-Dimension Assessment. Quality scored 9 out of 10 — no surprise. Maintenance was solid at 7. Production planning looked good on paper at 6.

But here's what the assessment revealed: a hidden dependency.

Three departments — stamping, assembly, and finishing — each had their own scheduling logic. Each optimized locally. And their schedules conflicted.

Every day, the floor supervisor spent 2-3 hours 'firefighting' — manually adjusting what ran when, negotiating with department heads, expediting orders.

This wasn't a technology problem. It was a coordination problem."

### The Solution (30 sec)

"The fix was surprisingly simple.

We implemented a shared scheduling protocol. One weekly meeting. One master schedule. Clear rules for priority conflicts.

No new software. No expensive integration. Just alignment.

Within 8 weeks, OEE hit 80%. The 12-point improvement came almost entirely from eliminating changeover delays and reducing WIP."

### Key Lesson (20 sec)

"The lesson? Before investing in technology, understand your dependencies.

The DRD methodology identified what years of operational data missed: the friction between departments was the constraint, not the equipment or the people."

## Summary (15 sec)

"This automotive supplier improved OEE by 12 points in 8 weeks by addressing one hidden scheduling dependency. No new machines. No new software. Just clarity about where the real problem was."
