## Avatar Script

[INTRO - 0:00-0:45]
"Before rolling out a transformation initiative across your entire organization, you need to test it. Pilots let you validate your approach, learn what works, and refine before scaling. In this video, I'll show you how to plan, execute, and learn from pilot programs using Consultify's Implementation module."

[SECTION 1: What is a Pilot? - 0:45-1:45]
"A pilot is a controlled implementation of your initiative with a limited scope – perhaps one department, one site, one process, or one product line.

The purpose is to validate assumptions, identify problems, measure actual results, and build organizational confidence before committing to full-scale rollout.

Good pilots are designed as experiments. You have hypotheses about what will work, you test them in controlled conditions, you collect data, and you adjust based on findings.

Pilots reduce risk. If something doesn't work, you've only affected a small part of your organization. You can fix it before broader deployment."

[SECTION 2: Planning Your Pilot - 1:45-3:15]
"In the Implementation module, select an initiative and click 'Plan Pilot'. You'll configure several elements.

First, define the pilot scope. Where will you test? Choose a representative but manageable area. It should be complex enough to surface real challenges, but contained enough to manage closely.

Next, set your success criteria. What metrics will tell you the pilot succeeded? Be specific: 'Reduce process time by 20%' rather than 'improve efficiency.' You'll measure these at the end.

Define your pilot duration. Pilots should be long enough to see real results but short enough to maintain urgency. Typically four to twelve weeks, depending on initiative complexity.

Identify pilot participants. Who will be directly involved? You need people willing to try new things and provide honest feedback. Include skeptics – they'll find problems others miss.

Finally, plan your data collection. What will you measure? How often? Who's responsible? Build measurement into the pilot design, not as an afterthought."

[SECTION 3: Setting Up in Consultify - 3:15-4:30]
"Once your plan is ready, click 'Create Pilot' to set it up in the system. Fill in the configuration details from your planning.

The pilot dashboard appears, showing progress, key metrics, issues raised, and lessons learned. This is your control center for the pilot period.

Add your team members to the pilot. They'll have access to report progress, log issues, and share observations.

Set up your measurement schedule. Consultify will remind participants when it's time to record metrics, conduct surveys, or update status."

[SECTION 4: Running the Pilot - 4:30-6:00]
"During the pilot, several activities are critical.

Daily or weekly check-ins keep momentum. Use the pilot discussion board to share observations, ask questions, and solve problems quickly.

Track your defined metrics religiously. The metrics dashboard shows trends over time – you'll see if things are improving or struggling.

Log issues as they arise. Every problem is a learning opportunity. The issues log captures what went wrong, why, and how it was addressed.

Conduct mid-pilot reviews. At the halfway point, assess progress. Are you on track to meet success criteria? Do you need to adjust the approach?

Document everything. Lessons learned, workarounds, unexpected benefits, participant feedback – all of this informs your rollout strategy."

[SECTION 5: Engaging Pilot Participants - 6:00-7:00]
"The people in your pilot are crucial. They're your early adopters, your testers, and your future champions.

Regular communication keeps them engaged. Share updates, celebrate small wins, and acknowledge challenges honestly.

Collect feedback continuously. Quick pulse surveys, informal conversations, feedback forms – multiple channels capture different perspectives.

Recognize contributions. Pilot participants are taking on extra work and risk. Appreciate them, and they'll become advocates.

Address concerns promptly. If participants struggle, help them. Their success equals your pilot's success."

[SECTION 6: Evaluating Pilot Results - 7:00-8:15]
"As the pilot concludes, it's evaluation time. Consultify generates a pilot report comparing results against success criteria.

Review each metric: Did you achieve target improvements? If not, why? What factors contributed to success or shortfall?

Analyze the issues log: Which problems were resolved? Which persist? What systemic issues emerged?

Gather final feedback: Conduct exit interviews or surveys with participants. What worked? What would they change? Would they recommend rollout?

Assess readiness for scale: Based on evidence, is the initiative ready for broader deployment? What refinements are needed first?"

[SECTION 7: Decision Points - 8:15-9:00]
"After evaluation, you face a decision. The Pilot Review meeting brings stakeholders together to decide next steps.

If the pilot clearly succeeded – metrics met, issues manageable, participants positive – the decision is likely: proceed to rollout.

If results are mixed, you might refine and run another pilot cycle, perhaps in a different context or with adjusted approach.

If the pilot failed, you have valuable information. Understand why, decide whether to redesign significantly, pivot to an alternative approach, or cancel the initiative.

Whatever the decision, document it thoroughly. The pilot findings become part of your organizational knowledge."

[SECTION 8: Transitioning to Rollout - 9:00-9:35]
"If you're proceeding, click 'Transition to Rollout' to move from pilot to deployment phase. Consultify carries over your pilot learnings, refined processes, and success metrics.

Your pilot participants become rollout champions – they've lived the change and can help others adopt.

The transition wizard helps you scale: What needs to change for broader deployment? What training is required? How will you sequence the rollout?"

[OUTRO - 9:35-10:00]
"Pilots are your safety net. They let you fail small and succeed big. Take them seriously, learn from them thoroughly, and your full rollouts will be dramatically more successful.

Next, we'll explore the Stage-Gate process that governs how initiatives move through implementation phases. Let's keep going!"
