- **Created**: 2026-01-31
- **Last updated**: 2026-01-31

---

## 1. Purpose

The experience curve is the hypothesis that **unit costs decline by a characteristic percentage each time cumulative production doubles**. It supports strategic decisions about pricing, scale, and market share.

---

## 2. Method (step-by-step)

1. Define the unit of output and cost scope (include full delivered cost).\n2) Gather historical cost and cumulative volume data.\n3) Fit an experience curve and estimate learning rate.\n4) Compare with competitors (relative experience).\n5) Translate into strategic implications: pricing, investment, capacity, sourcing.\n6) Convert into initiatives (cost reduction programs, volume strategy, sourcing).

---

## 3. Outputs & DoD

- Learning rate estimate\n- Cost projection by volume\n- Strategic options (price/volume/share)\n- Initiatives and risk notes\n
  DoD:\n- [ ] Data collected and normalized\n- [ ] Curve fitted with assumptions\n- [ ] Options evaluated\n- [ ] Initiatives created

---

## 4. UI / Graphic spec

- Log-log chart: unit cost vs cumulative volume\n- Scenario toggles (learning rate ranges)\n- Competitor overlay (relative curves)\n- Export: chart + assumptions PDF

---

## 5. Worked example

Battery manufacturing: learning rate 22% per doubling; forecast cost advantage with scale; initiatives: capacity ramp, yield improvement, supplier terms.
